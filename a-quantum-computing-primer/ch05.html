Amplitudes & Phases

Phase is physically real, computationally essential — and invisible until you rotate it.

Amplitudes have magnitudes and phases. Magnitudes decide measurement statistics directly. Phases decide nothing directly — and everything indirectly, because interference adds amplitudes before squaring them, so aligned phases reinforce and opposed phases cancel.

Global phase: physically meaningless

The states |ψ⟩ and e^{iθ}|ψ⟩ are indistinguishable by any measurement in any basis. An overall phase factors out of every probability. It is notation, not physics.

Relative phase: physically real, but hidden

Compare:

|+⟩ = ( |0⟩ + |1⟩ ) / √2      |−⟩ = ( |0⟩ − |1⟩ ) / √2

notation — teal: amplitude · violet: phase

Measured in the standard basis, both give a 50/50 coin — indistinguishable. But apply a Hadamard first: H|+⟩ = |0⟩ and H|−⟩ = |1⟩. One shot now separates them perfectly. The phase difference was real all along; the Hadamard rotated the measurement basis so that phase became amplitude.

Phase as the working currency of computation

This is not a curiosity — it is the standard trick. Oracles in Grover's algorithm mark the answer by flipping its phase, leaving all magnitudes untouched and the state looking (to a standard-basis measurement) exactly as flat as before. The period structure in Shor's algorithm lives entirely in phases after modular exponentiation. In both cases a subsequent operation — diffusion, inverse QFT — cashes the phase pattern out into measurable amplitude.

Keep this Global phase: gauge freedom, ignore it. Relative phase: hidden state, recoverable by rotating the basis. Algorithms write answers in phase because phase is where you can compute without disturbing the statistics — then convert exactly once, at the end.
