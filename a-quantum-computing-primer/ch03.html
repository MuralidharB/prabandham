Superposition & Entanglement

From one qubit in two states at once to two qubits with no individual states at all.

Superposition is a single system carrying amplitude over multiple outcomes at once. Entanglement is what happens when superposition spans multiple systems: the joint state is definite, but the parts no longer have states of their own.

The Bell pair

The canonical entangled state of two qubits:

|Φ⁺⟩ = ( |00⟩ + |11⟩ ) / √2

Half the amplitude on “both zero,” half on “both one,” and — crucially — nothing on |01⟩ or |10⟩. Measure either qubit and you get a random bit; but the other qubit's outcome is then fixed to match, regardless of distance.

Non-factorability is the definition

Try to write |Φ⁺⟩ as (a|0⟩+b|1⟩) ⊗ (c|0⟩+d|1⟩). Expanding gives ac|00⟩ + ad|01⟩ + bc|10⟩ + bd|11⟩. Killing the cross terms forces ad = bc = 0, which then kills either the |00⟩ or the |11⟩ term too. No factorization exists. That failure is entanglement: correlation that cannot be decomposed into separate local descriptions.

The two-gate recipe

Start at |00⟩. Hadamard on qubit A spreads amplitude: (|00⟩+|10⟩)/√2. CNOT with A as control copies the correlation: (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2. Spread, then correlate — two gates, and you have the unit currency of entanglement.

Four Bell states, one basis

Varying the sign and the pairing gives |Φ⁻⟩ = (|00⟩−|11⟩)/√2, |Ψ⁺⟩ = (|01⟩+|10⟩)/√2, and |Ψ⁻⟩ = (|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2. Together the four form an orthonormal basis for the two-qubit space — the working alphabet of teleportation, superdense coding, and entanglement-based QKD.

Scaling up The register states inside Shor's algorithm — where a control register becomes correlated with the values of modular exponentiation — are this same phenomenon at scale. A Bell pair is the two-qubit special case of the correlations every serious quantum algorithm builds and then harvests through interference.
