Gates: The Quantum Instruction Set

Every quantum program is a sequence of rotations. A handful of them suffice.

Quantum gates are unitary matrices — reversible, length-preserving linear maps on the state vector. Geometrically, every single-qubit gate is a rotation of the Bloch sphere. There is no “if,” no “write,” no erase: just rotations, composed.

The working set

GateActionRole
X|0⟩ ↔ |1⟩Bit flip (quantum NOT)
Z|1⟩ → −|1⟩Phase flip — invisible in the standard basis, decisive under interference
H|0⟩ → |+⟩, |1⟩ → |−⟩Superposition maker and basis rotator — the phase-to-amplitude workhorse
S, T|1⟩ → i|1⟩, e^{iπ/4}|1⟩Finer phase rotations; T unlocks universality
CNOTFlip target iff control is |1⟩The entangler; correlation between qubits

Universality

Just as NAND suffices for classical logic, a small set — for instance {H, T, CNOT} — can approximate any n-qubit unitary to arbitrary precision. Every algorithm in Part III compiles down to circuits over a set like this.

Reading a circuit

Time flows left to right; each wire is a qubit; gates are boxes on wires; a measurement symbol terminates a wire into a classical bit. The Hadamard deserves special respect in this notation: applied across a whole register of |0⟩s, it produces the uniform superposition over all 2ⁿ strings — step one of nearly every algorithm you will meet.

Keep this X moves amplitude between outcomes; Z moves phase between components; H converts between the two currencies. Most circuit intuition is bookkeeping over those three sentences.
