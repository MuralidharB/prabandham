What on-prem actually costs

What you actually install

Specified, not yet built

The chart below is the shape agreed in the engineering spec, not a published artifact. Treat it as what an install will look like, and do not promise a delivery date from this page. Everything the chart depends on already exists in the product — the two services share nothing but the database, and schema migrations apply themselves on worker start.

  provider: bedrock               # bedrock | anthropic | vertex
  region: us-east-1
  serviceAccountRoleArn: arn:aws:iam::123456789012:role/git2docs-bedrock

git:
  provider: github                # github | github-enterprise | gitlab
  baseUrl: https://github.example.com/api/v3
  appId: "123456"

    secretName: git2docs-github

How long until it is doing something useful

30–60 min
Chart to working portal, on a cluster where the prerequisites above are already met. Migrations apply themselves when the worker boots, so there is no schema step.
Hours
First documentation set. Generation is model work proportional to the size of the repository, running as a background job. Nobody sits and watches it, but do not promise a live demo twenty minutes after install.
2–4 weeks
Realistic calendar time inside an enterprise, and almost none of it is the chart: IAM roles, the DNS name, the identity provider, the egress allowlist, the security questionnaire, and getting a GitHub App approved on their own instance. Plan the engagement around those, not around the install.
Ongoing
Someone operates it. Postgres backups, chart upgrades on our release cadence, and a single worker replica that scales by getting bigger rather than by multiplying. That person's time is not on the cost chart below and belongs in the business case.

Bedrock is not the cheaper option, and selling it as one will go wrong at renewal. Per token it costs the same or slightly more, plus about $15–22 a month for the private endpoint. What the customer buys is the data boundary, one invoice, and no API key sitting in their cluster. For a security review, that is usually worth more than the difference.

Three customers, three answers

“Source can't leave our network”

No product to sell. Comprehension and synthesis run on Claude; that is the product, not a configuration flag. Disqualify on the first call rather than at the demo.

“No third party may hold our data”

Bedrock in their own account, hosted control plane. Days of engineering, no chart, no release tax. This satisfies more procurement reviews than it sounds like it should — lead with it.

“It runs on our infrastructure”

The full install: Helm chart, their EKS, their RDS, their change control. Weeks to build, and a permanent cost on every release after that. Price it accordingly.

Say this part out loud

Self-managing costs several times the hosted subscription, before anyone writes a page. On-prem is a control purchase, not a savings purchase — a customer who expects it to be cheaper has misunderstood what they are buying.

  • Documentation text still reaches Voyage for embeddings, even on Bedrock. Voyage also publishes model packages on AWS Marketplace that run in the customer's own VPC — confirm the model and its output dimension before offering that as a closed loop.
  • Someone has to operate it. The numbers here are AWS invoice only. A platform engineer's time on upgrades, backups and incidents is the larger cost and it belongs in the business case.
  • Versions drift. A self-managed customer runs whatever they last upgraded to, and will report bugs that the hosted build fixed months ago. Put the upgrade cadence in the contract.
  • The worker runs as a single replica. Generation throughput scales by making that node bigger, not by adding more of them.

Infrastructure figures are AWS list prices for us-east-1, on demand, retrieved November 2026 — verify in the AWS Pricing Calculator before quoting. EKS $0.10/cluster-hour, NAT gateway $0.045/hour plus data, ALB $16.20/month plus LCUs, public IPv4 $3.65/month, RDS db.m7g.large PostgreSQL about $0.168/hour. Model prices are Claude Sonnet 4.6 at $3.00 / $15.00 per million tokens, cache reads at $0.30. Token volume per page is an internal working assumption — 45,000 input and 10,000 output — and should be recalibrated from a real customer run before it appears in a quote. Reserved instances, Savings Plans and an AWS enterprise discount all reduce the infrastructure column materially.
